RRole-Based Discovery Question BankUse when different stakeholders join the call.▼

Quality / NDT Level III

  • What acceptance standard or internal procedure controls the decision?
  • What IQI / SRb / CNR / SNR evidence must be documented?
  • What defect types have actually escaped or caused debate?
  • Do you need DICONDE, review audit trail, annotations, measurements, or image archive?
  • Who approves a technique change from film / CR to DDA or CT?

Manufacturing / Operations

  • How many parts per shift and what cycle time is acceptable?
  • Where does inspection occur in the process flow?
  • What happens to accepted, rejected, and suspect parts?
  • Manual loading, crane loading, conveyor, robot, or mixed?
  • What would make the system a bottleneck?

Engineering / R&D

  • What feature or failure mode are you trying to understand?
  • Do you need depth, volume, metrology, CAD comparison, or only detection?
  • Are samples representative and do they include known defects?
  • What minimum feature size matters, and in what orientation?
  • Do you have CAD / STP, drawings, or prior images?

Production / Automation

  • Do you need recipe-based inspection or operator-driven review?
  • Do you require ADR, marking, sorting, PLC handshakes, or MES integration?
  • Which PLC / robot standard is preferred?
  • What data must move back to production?
  • What uptime, spare parts, and service response are expected?

EHS / Facilities / RSO

  • Where will the system be installed?
  • Who owns radiation safety registration and state requirements?
  • Any ceiling height, door, floor load, power, HVAC, or access constraints?
  • Cabinet system or permanent radiographic installation / vault?
  • Any customer-specific safety devices, light curtains, scanners, or access controls?

Procurement / Economic Buyer

  • Is budget allocated or still being requested?
  • What is the quote deadline and desired validity?
  • Are Incoterms, payment milestones, tariffs, or vendor setup already defined?
  • Who signs the PO and who can block it?
  • What other vendors are being evaluated and by what criteria?

Density-correction rule of thumb: Convert unfamiliar alloys to steel-equivalent thickness. Approximate ratios vs. steel: Al ≈ 0.35, Ti ≈ 0.6, Inconel ≈ 1.1–1.2, Cu ≈ 1.1, W ≈ 2.4. Always validate when quoting detection commitments.
Sales caution: A customer may state “225 kV works today,” but that may be film/CR, long exposure, low throughput, or limited acceptance. Ask for current technique, exposure time, IQI, and example images before routing.

Detector technology types

a-Si DDA

Amorphous-silicon flat panel. Large active areas, common in industrial DR, strong for castings, welds, and general cabinet systems. Pair with CsI or GOS/Gadox scintillator.

CMOS DDA

Smaller active area, high speed and high MTF potential. Strong for small parts, electronics, fine AM features, and high-magnification cabinet/CT use where FOV is acceptable.

Scintillator Choice

CsI = needle structure, higher DQE/sharpness, often preferred for aerospace-style image quality. GOS/Gadox = more rugged/economical and can be attractive for high-energy production work.

Direct / Photon Counting

Direct-conversion or photon-counting DDAs, including THOR-style panels where applicable, can offer energy discrimination and high contrast. Confirm energy range, count-rate limits, active area, and industrial integration maturity before recommending.

Detector selection note: do not treat pixel pitch as the only decision. Confirm active area, energy rating, scintillator, frame rate, DQE/MTF, saturation behavior, country of origin/customer restrictions, software integration, and required image-quality evidence.

F Modality Selection — DR vs. CT vs. Laminography ▼

Product routing picks the system; this picks the method. Recommend the right tool, not the most expensive one — over-selling CT kills trust, under-selling it risks an escape. The most valuable judgment you offer is the honest fit.

RecommendWhen it fits
2D DR is enoughDefect is volumetric / high-contrast (gas porosity, FOD, gross inclusions) and not orientation-dependent; one or a few views capture it; throughput is paramount and geometry is simple. Sell DR with confidence and frame CT as a future upgrade path.
Laminography (CL) fitsPart is flat / board-like (PCB, thin composite, planar assembly) where full rotation is impractical; you need layer separation by depth but not a metrology-grade volume; limited-angle scan is far faster than full CT on a large panel.
CT becomes necessaryCritical defect is planar and orientation-dependent (cracks, lack of fusion) — 2D misses it at the wrong angle; features overlap in projection and must be separated by depth; defect must be characterized in 3D (size/shape/location), not just detected; internal geometry must be seen; process feedback needs 3D defect maps (classic in AM).
Metrology CT is requiredDeliverable is a traceable dimensional measurement with documented uncertainty, not pass/fail; a standard demands it (VDA 5, ISO 10360, PPAP, first-article of complex parts); internal-and-external dimensions must correlate to CAD and to the CMM. Different build: calibrated stage, SNR ≥ 20, rigorous surface determination, beam-hardening control — qualify this early so you spec and price for it.
AI / ADR creates valueVolume is high enough that manual review can't scale or stay consistent; false-call rate is scrapping good parts; defect types are well-characterized with labeled data to train and validate; customer needs consistent, auditable, traceable decisions for regulated parts.
Know your technique limits — disbonds & low-contrast defects

A disbond is adhesive joint failure with no physical gap — no density change means CT contrast may be too low to detect it reliably. Similarly, some composite delaminations produce only marginal density contrast. If a customer asks "can you find our disbonds?" the honest answer is: depends on whether there's a density gap. Steering them toward complementary methods (ultrasonic, thermography) when X-ray isn't the right fit is itself a selling skill — it builds the trust that wins the broader relationship and avoids a failed demo. Offer a feasibility scan before committing.

POD · False calls · Escape risk
TermWhat it is & how to use it
PODProbability of detection — likelihood of catching a given defect. Aerospace benchmark is a90/95 (90% detection at 95% confidence). Tie your capability to their POD requirement with demo data, not claims.
FCR / false callsShare of good parts wrongly rejected. A commonly cited target is <2% for production casting/automotive; the right threshold depends entirely on part value, volume, and the customer's scrap economics — validate on their parts, not a generic benchmark. High FCR destroys ROI and operator trust.
Escape riskCost-weighted probability a defect reaches the customer — the executive's real fear. Connect detection capability to escapes prevented in dollars and contract/brand risk.
The POD/FCR balance: they pull against each other — crank sensitivity up and you catch more defects but reject more good parts. The art (and where AI-ADR earns its keep) is raising POD while holding FCR low. Frame your value as winning both sides, proven on the customer's own parts in a pilot.

1. Modalities & Methods
NDT / NDE — Non-Destructive Testing / Evaluation. Umbrella term for inspection without damaging the part (RT, UT, MT, PT, ET, VT).
RT — Radiographic Testing; X-ray or gamma, the NDT method this workbook is about.
DR / 2D DR — Digital Radiography; fast single-shot, no depth info. Best for FOD, welds, inline sorting.
CR — Computed Radiography; image plate + scanner, bridge between film and DDA. Slower, lower resolution than DR.
CT / 3D CT — Computed Tomography; full 360° volume reconstruction. Metrology + internal defects, slower than 2D.
2.5D / CL — Computed Laminography; limited rotation (±20–40°), separates layers in flat parts (PCBs, panels).
4D CT — CT over time or under load. In-situ crack growth, thermal cycling studies.
In-situ CT — CT while part is under mechanical/thermal/chemical stress. Research & material science.
Helical / Helix CT — Continuous spiral scan; tall parts, higher throughput vs. step-and-shoot.
Offset CT — Detector offset to double the effective FOV. For oversized parts.
Fan-beam vs. Cone-beam — Fan = slice-by-slice via line detector (less scatter, slower). Cone = volumetric via flat panel (faster, more scatter).
FDK (Feldkamp) — Default cone-beam reconstruction algorithm (filtered back-projection variant).
FBP — Filtered back-projection; fast, noisier baseline CT algorithm.
Iterative Recon (ART / SART / MBIR) — Family of CT reconstruction algorithms that refine the 3D volume estimate through repeated passes, unlike filtered back-projection (FBP/FDK) which is a single-pass calculation. Iterative methods handle noisy, incomplete, or limited-projection data significantly better, at the cost of more computation time.
ART (Algebraic Reconstruction Technique) — The original iterative approach. Updates the volume one projection at a time, correcting the estimate each pass until it converges. Mathematically rigorous but slow; the foundation everything else builds on.
SART (Simultaneous Algebraic Reconstruction Technique) — All projections update the volume simultaneously rather than sequentially. Faster convergence than ART, better noise suppression, more practical for industrial use. When vendors say "iterative reconstruction," SART-family is usually what's running.
MBIR (Model-Based Iterative Reconstruction) — The most powerful modern variant. Incorporates a statistical model of the imaging system (detector noise, beam geometry, scatter) plus a regularization prior — typically total variation, which suppresses noise while preserving edges. The result: equivalent image quality with 50–80% fewer projections. This is the physics behind sparse-view CT and fast-acquisition modes. Sales point: when a customer says CT is too slow for their line, MBIR + sparse-view is the answer.
AI Recon — Learned denoise / sparse-view reconstruction; faster + cleaner, emerging standard.
2. X-Ray Sources & Physics
kV / kVp — Tube voltage / peak kilovoltage = penetration. Higher kV = thicker/denser material, lower contrast.
mA / Power (W) — Tube current = flux. More mA = more photons, better SNR, more heat.
Exposure — mA × time = total signal dose delivered to detector.
Focal Spot (f) — Source size. Small = sharp + low power; large = power + blur.
Focus types (nano/micro/mini/meso/macro) — From <1 µm (nano, research) through µm (micro) to hundreds of µm (meso/macro, high-power).
Reflection Target — Standard tube type; high power, large focal spot.
Transmission Target — Microfocus tube type; focal spot <5 µm, low power.
LINAC — Linear accelerator; accelerates electrons to generate high-energy X-rays. Industrial range broadly 1–15+ MeV; diondo d7 offers 3 / 6 / 9 MeV. Required only for very thick or very dense cross-sections where kV systems cannot penetrate. Do not default to LINAC for titanium at normal thicknesses — confirm with applications engineering.
Filtration — Cu/Al added; hardens beam, cuts low-energy scatter, reduces dose.
Dual Energy — Two kV spectra; material discrimination, better FOD contrast.
COMET — Swiss X-ray tube manufacturer; VCxray's standard partner for 160–450 kV tubes.
Beam Hardening — False density gradients (cupping/streaks) from polychromatic beam absorbed unevenly.
JIMA — Japan Inspection Instruments Manufacturers' Association; standard resolution target for microfocus tubes.
3. Detectors & Image Chain
DDA / FPD — Digital Detector Array / Flat-Panel Detector; CMOS or a-Si based.
a-Si (detector) — Amorphous silicon; large active area, high DQE, robust at high energies.
CMOS (detector) — Fast, small pixel, high MTF; smaller active area than a-Si.
Line detector — 1D array; fan-beam, low scatter, slower than FPD. Used in d4/d5/d7.
CsI Scintillator — Needle crystal structure; sharp, high MTF.
GOS Scintillator — Gadolinium oxysulfide; brighter, cheaper, lower spatial resolution than CsI.
Pixel Pitch — Detector element size; 50–200 µm typical.
Bit Depth — 14–16 bit = 16k–65k grey levels. More bits = more contrast steps.
Imaging Chain — Tube + filter + part + detector combination; sizing balances penetration, resolution, throughput.
Stitching / Subvolume — Combining multiple partial scans when part exceeds detector FOV.
4. Performance Metrics & Geometry
M = SDD/SOD — Geometric magnification. Higher M = higher resolution, more blur risk, smaller FOV.
SDD — Source-to-detector distance.
SOD — Source-to-object distance.
Ug = f(M−1) — Geometric unsharpness; keep below detector resolution.
EPS / SRb — Effective Pixel Size / Basic Spatial Resolution; true system resolution at part plane.
Voxel Size — 3D pixel; CT resolution set by magnification and detector pitch.
VOI — Volume Of Interest; sub-region within a CT dataset for targeted analysis.
MTF — Modulation Transfer Function; sharpness vs. spatial frequency.
DQE — Detective Quantum Efficiency; low-dose performance benchmark.
SNR — Signal-to-Noise Ratio; ≥5 to detect, ≥20 to measure.
CNR — Contrast-to-Noise Ratio; defect visibility metric, often more important than SNR in practice.
Contrast Sensitivity — Smallest % thickness change visible; per ASTM E2698.
Dynamic Range — Ratio of brightest to darkest level captured without saturation.
IQI — Image Quality Indicator; wire/duplex reference for daily performance check (see Appendix C for 2-2T / 1-2T details).
POD — Probability of Detection; a90/95 is aerospace gold standard.
5. Defects & Features
Cracks — Planar, orientation-sensitive; hardest to detect in 2D. CT sees all angles.
Gas Porosity — Round low-density voids; trapped gas during solidification or welding.
Shrinkage Porosity — Dendritic/irregular voids; solidification shrinkage in castings.
Keyhole Porosity — AM-specific; spherical, from high-energy laser/e-beam process.
Lack of Fusion (LoF) — AM + weld critical; planar gap, worst-case for 2D orientation.
Cold Lap / Misrun — Casting flow defect; premature solidification, linear seam or underfill.
Inclusions — Embedded foreign material; slag, oxides, tungsten in welds.
Delamination — Layer separation in composites, PCBs, laminates.
FOD — Foreign Object Debris; metallic/non-metallic contamination inside assembly.
Wire Sweep / Solder Voids — Electronics; bond wire displaced or solder joint void.
Wall Thickness Analysis — Colour map of min/max wall; replaces destructive sectioning.
Porosity Analysis — Automated void characterization & statistics.
6. Artifacts (CT Image Problems)
Ring Artifacts — Concentric rings in CT; detector pixel gain/offset calibration drift.
Metal Streaking / Starburst — Lines from dense material; photon starvation in projection.
MAR — Metal Artifact Reduction algorithm; reduces starburst streaks from dense inserts.
Scatter Fog — Contrast loss across image; cone-beam geometry, thick/large parts.
Scatter Correction / diScatter — Restores contrast lost in cone-beam CT due to secondary photons.
Aliasing — Jagged edges / false features from too few projections (undersampling).
Truncation — Part larger than detector FOV; clips projections, distorts reconstruction.
Motion Blur — Part shifts during exposure; manipulator vibration or loose fixturing.
Calibration Drift — Detector gain/geometry change over time; auto-cal routines mitigate.

10. Radiation Safety
ALARA — As Low As Reasonably Achievable; dose optimisation principle.
RSO — Radiation Safety Officer; designated person responsible for the radiation-safety program at a facility (required under 10 CFR / state rules for permanent installations).
mR / mrem / mSv — Exposure and dose units. 1 rem = 10 mSv = 1000 mrem. Cabinet limit: 0.5 mR integrated over any 1 hour at 5 cm (21 CFR 1020.40).

Interlock — Safety switch that disables x-rays when door/barrier is opened; required in cabinets and vaults.
Dose Rate — Radiation intensity at a point (mR/hr or µSv/hr); drives area posting category.

15. U.S. Safety, Certification & Installation Terms
Radiation-Producing Machine — Device that generates ionizing radiation electrically, such as an X-ray tube system or accelerator. For these systems, state radiation-control programs are usually the practical regulatory interface.
State Radiation-Control Program — State agency or program that registers, inspects, and regulates X-ray machines / radiation-producing machines. This is more directly relevant to cabinet/vault X-ray systems than Agreement State radioactive-material licensing.
Agreement State — NRC agreement status for regulating certain radioactive materials. Important for sealed-source/gamma work, but for X-ray tube systems the state still regulates radiation-producing machines whether or not the “Agreement State” label is the main issue.
UL Listing — Product or assembly listing by UL to an applicable safety standard. In custom industrial machinery, confirm whether the whole system, panel, component, or field evaluation is covered.
NRTL — Nationally Recognized Testing Laboratory. OSHA-recognized organization that tests/certifies equipment to U.S. safety standards where NRTL approval is required.
ETL / Intertek — Another NRTL mark often accepted similarly to UL when the certification scope and standard are appropriate.
Field Evaluation — On-site evaluation by an NRTL or qualified body when a custom system does not carry a complete listing acceptable to the AHJ or customer.
AHJ — Authority Having Jurisdiction. The entity that decides whether an installation satisfies local/electrical/building/radiation requirements.
Shielding Plan Review — State or customer review of shielding calculations/layout before vault construction or high-energy installation.
Radiation Survey — Measurement after installation or modification to verify dose rates at boundaries, doors, seams, and occupied areas.
